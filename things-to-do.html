Cool Things You Can Do With Bananas

1. Bananas and banana peels make great fertilizer (you can compost them, bury them whole, or cut them in small pieces and mix them with garden soil) because of their phosphorous and potassium content. Roses especially like them.
2. You can use the inside of a banana peel to clean and polish leather shoes.
3. Rubbing the inside of a banana peel on houseplant leaves makes the leaves shiny without harming them.
4. Banana peels also make a good silver polish—just rub silver with the inside of a peel and then buff with a cloth.
5. Thanks to its oil, rubbing the inside of a banana peel on a mosquito bite (or other bug bite) or on poison ivy will help keep it from itching and getting inflamed.
6. If you tape a banana peel over a splinter, the enzymes help the splinter work its way out of your skin (and also heal the wound).
7. Bananas make great conditioner. Mash a banana and add a tablespoon of heavy cream and a tablespoon of honey to the mixture. Then apply it to dry hair, cover your hair with a shower cap, and then wrap your head in a warm towel. Leave it on for up to an hour and then rinse thoroughly with warm water before shampooing.
8. Create a non-toxic trap by placing a chopped, overripe banana peel inside a small bowl or jar. Squirt a drop of dish soap on it, then wrap the top tightly with plastic wrap and poke a few small holes. Flies will crawl in for the sweet scent but won't be able to escape.
9. In environmental science, minced banana peels are used as an effective, low-cost water filter. The proteins and sugars in the peel contain atoms that naturally bind to heavy metal pollutants like copper and lead, helping to pull them out of contaminated water.
10. Place overripe banana slices or peels on a elevated platform or tray in your garden. The sweet, fermenting sugars are highly attractive to butterflies, bees, and fruit-eating birds.
